# Injury Returns and Waiver Timing

## Evidence Ladder

Rank signals by reliability:

1. Official squad involvement or verified full team training.
2. Specific manager or club update with an expected return window.
3. Partial or individual training and reliable local reporting.
4. Draft status, playing-chance or return-date improvement.
5. Aggregator estimates, social speculation or an unchanged generic return date.

Record the source date. A more recent lower-quality report does not automatically outweigh direct club evidence.

## Return Stages

Use these practical stages:

- `Rehabilitation`: No team training; monitor unless the player is exceptionally valuable.
- `Individual training`: Early stash only when upside is high and bench cost is low.
- `Partial team training`: Consider a pre-emptive waiver if rival interest is likely.
- `Full training`: Strong claim window, subject to role and recurrence risk.
- `Matchday squad`: Assess cameo likelihood and path to starts.
- `Managed return`: Value expected minutes rather than name recognition.
- `Starting normally`: Treat as an ordinary available player, retaining recurrence risk where relevant.

## Claim Now, Wait, or Avoid

Claim now when several of these are true:

- The player has meaningful top-end value at the position.
- Evidence has improved since the previous snapshot.
- Full training, squad inclusion or a near-term return is credible.
- The upcoming fixtures reward holding through one or two managed appearances.
- A rival has a clear positional need or has recently churned that position.
- Craig has a dispensable player or usable injured-reserve mechanism if the league supports one.

Wait for free agency when the player is unlikely to attract a claim, evidence is still weak, or spending a strong waiver priority would damage flexibility.

Avoid or deprioritise when the return date is repeatedly slipping, recurrence risk is high, the player has lost their role, the team has strong alternatives, or the required drop has more dependable value.

## Priority Framework

Classify each target:

- `Aggressive waiver`: High upside, credible near return and likely rival demand.
- `Standard waiver`: Clear squad improvement without needing the earliest priority.
- `Speculative late waiver`: Upside exists but evidence or minutes remain uncertain.
- `Free-agent watch`: Useful only if unclaimed after waivers.
- `Monitor`: No move yet; define the next evidence trigger.

Never recommend burning top waiver priority solely because an automated return date is close.

## Comparison Checklist

For each possible stash, compare:

- Expected points and minutes over two, four and six gameweeks
- Best-case and realistic return dates
- First-start estimate, which may differ from medical return
- Competition for the player's role
- Set pieces and role before versus after injury
- Fixture run at the likely first start
- Drop cost and bench congestion
- Rival demand and waiver-order cost
- Evidence freshness and confidence

The final recommendation should identify the trigger that would change the decision, such as full training, inclusion on the bench or a manager confirming readiness to start.
